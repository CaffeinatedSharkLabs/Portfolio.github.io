01The problem

Without a front door, intake is chaos.

Survey requests used to arrive by phone, by email and by voicemail. Details got lost, nobody was sure what had been promised, and clients called back to ask whether anyone had received their request. Once the job started, those gaps showed up in billing and scheduling.

  • Voicemail

    "…need corners staked before grading, two parcels, call me back…"No address. No callback number.

  • Email

    "Following up on my request from last week. Did you get it?"Which request?

  • Phone

    A quote promised "by Friday," written on a sticky note.Sticky note missing.

  • Web form

    A survey request typed into the site's generic contact form.Free text, not a record.

A kudurru was a Babylonian boundary stone: a carved record of who held a parcel and where its lines ran.

The name fits. Kudurru Stone is the system of record for a survey firm, and the web client is where that record is kept: every request, project, crew assignment and schedule change in one place. Crews carry the .NET MAUI field app on site.

03Form builder

Not a generic contact form. Their form, as many as they need.

A firm names a form, adds fields, marks each one required or optional, previews it and publishes. A quote request, a boundary survey and an ALTA can each have their own form and shareable link, showing the firm's logo, colors and welcome message.

    04The flow

    From a request to a job, with nothing retyped.

    Four steps, and the details the client typed in carry all the way through to the project.

    A

    Build your own request form

    Name it, then add text, email, phone, dropdown, checkbox or description fields, each required or optional. Preview it, then publish. A firm can run as many forms as it likes.

    B

    Share one link

    Each form has its own URL on the branded portal, with the firm's logo, colors and welcome message. It can go on the firm's website or into an email to a client.

    C

    Every submission gets a reference

    The client sees a confirmation with a tracking reference and gets a confirmation email. The team gets an internal notification with every field the client filled in.

    D

    Turn a request into a job

    The request becomes a project with its own project number, and can sync to QuickBooks if the firm wants it to. Nobody retypes what the client already entered.

    05Inside the firm

    Once it's a job, the whole office runs on it.

    The rest of the web client is where the firm works the job: who it's for, who's on it, when it happens and where it stands. Choose a screen, then select a numbered marker to read its note. Click a screenshot to enlarge it.

      06Visual language

      One meaning per color, on every screen.

      Green is underway or available, amber is waiting or partial, and gray is not started or unavailable. Task types keep their colors from the scheduler through to the project page, so the office reads status before it reads words.

      07Why it matters

      A request becomes a record, not another email.

      The portal runs on the same system as the firm's projects. A request arrives as a structured record with a reference number, so it becomes a project in one step, and clients can come back later and sign in to follow their projects.

      Generic contact form

      • Free text in an inbox
      • No reference number, so clients call to check on it
      • Someone retypes the details into the job system
      • Files go back and forth as email attachments
      • Same form for every kind of survey

      Kudurru Stone client portal

      • A structured record with every field in place
      • Tracking reference on screen and by email, with no login needed
      • Converts to a project in one step, with optional QuickBooks sync
      • Clients sign in with a passwordless magic link and swap files in dedicated folders
      • A separate branded form for each kind of request
